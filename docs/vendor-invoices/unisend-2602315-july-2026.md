## Billing basis

Two conventions had to be pinned down before the counts could be compared:

1. **Lane code = billing account → destination, not physical origin.** `createShippingLabel`
   (`src/lib/services/unisend/shipping.ts`) hardcodes `sender.address.countryCode: 'LV'` —
   the platform's own Rīga address — for every parcel regardless of seller country. So every
   parcel registers as LV-origin and the lane code's second country is the destination
   terminal. Same reading as the June invoice (`june_2026_entry_69` narrative).
2. **Billed on dispatch (first carrier scan), not on label registration.** `shipped_at` is
   written by the `sync-tracking` cron off the carrier scan. Matching on `shipped_at` produces
   an exact match on both lines; matching on `accepted_at` (label creation) does not.

## Follow-up (not blocking payment)

The hardcoded LV sender address means parcels physically injected in EE or LT are registered and
billed as LV-origin. Two of July's seven (WRRJ from a LT seller, 4CUP from an EE seller) were
billed at the €1.50 LV-LV rate while the buyer was charged €2.50 and €3.20 respectively for a
cross-border lane. It works in our favour today, but it is a contract-exposure question worth
raising with Unisend rather than leaving implicit — if they ever reconcile physical injection
point against registered sender, back-billing is the obvious remedy.
